Help

1. Video & projects

Load a video file from your computer. Nothing is uploaded; all processing happens locally.

Save project writes your frame-rate settings, reference length, and tracked points to a .json file (not the video itself). Load project restores them — reload the same video file first.

2. Frame rate

Either trust a known FPS from the file's properties, or estimate it from a burned-in overlay clock: step or play (not the seek bar or ±10 ms nudge, which don't preserve frame-accurate position) to a frame with a readable timestamp, and add a sample. Add samples spread widely apart (10+ seconds) so occasional dropped or duplicated frames average out in the fit rather than skewing a single interval. The R² value flags a bad fit — typically a mistyped sample or inconsistent footage.

3. Reference length

Click the same two points tracked as Track A and Track B (e.g. front & rear tire contact patches), at any one frame, and enter their known real-world distance apart — typically the vehicle's wheelbase.

This single length is all the cross-ratio method needs. Unlike a plain pixel-scale calibration, it does not need re-measuring as the vehicle's apparent size changes on screen, because the cross-ratio of collinear points is invariant under that kind of projective change. Adding more than one measurement just averages them for robustness.

4. Point tracking

Track A and Track B should be two points on the same rigid body (e.g. front and rear wheel), tracked at matching frames — the "Lock" option switches the active track after each click so both land on the same frame automatically. Click a table row to jump to that frame.

5. Speed results

Vehicle speed is computed via the cross-ratio method (see the citation in the footer), from Track A + Track B pairs at matching frames and the reference length above — not from a per-frame pixel scale, so it isn't thrown off by the vehicle's changing apparent size.

Each interval is checked for how closely its 4 points fall on a straight line (the "Straightness" column). Intervals that deviate too much — typically because the vehicle was turning — are shown dimmed and excluded from the overall average.

Frame accuracy

Prev/Next Frame stepping lands on real decoded video frames (via requestVideoFrameCallback, where the browser supports it) rather than assuming a frame rate. The total frame count is established once per video by a background scan on load. Releasing the seek bar after a drag triggers a quick automatic resync to recover the exact frame number; the ±10 ms nudge skips that resync for fast sub-frame alignment with the zoom loupe, so its frame counter reads "?" until you step or play again.
